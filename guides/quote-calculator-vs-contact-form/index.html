Quote calculator or contact form: which gets you better enquiries?

How an instant quote calculator compares with a plain contact form for small businesses: what customers see, what you receive, and when each one makes sense.

By Liam Hockley, AiEngineered. Updated 24 October 2026.

Short answer: a contact form asks people to wait for a price; a quote calculator shows them a guide price first and sends you their details with the job, size and budget already filled in. If your customers usually ask “how much?” before anything else, a calculator tends to suit them better.

Side by side

Contact formQuote calculator
What the customer seesEmpty boxes and “we’ll get back to you”A guide price for their job, straight away
What you receiveName, contact details and a free-text messageName, contact details, the job, the size or option, the guide price shown and notes
Price expectationsUnknown until you replySet before they contact you
Best forWork that needs a conversation before any priceJobs that can be priced roughly from a few choices

Why a price first changes the enquiry

Someone who sends a request after seeing a guide price has already accepted the rough cost, so your first reply can be about the job, not about whether they can afford it. People whose budget is far off usually don’t send a request at all, which saves you time.

When a contact form is still the right choice

  • Bespoke work where there is no sensible typical price.
  • Work where you need to ask several questions before you can say anything about cost.
  • General enquiries that aren’t about price.

Getting the most from a calculator

  • Keep the choices few and plain: the job, the size, and when they need it.
  • Use realistic ranges and say clearly that they are guides.
  • Reply quickly. A customer who has just seen a price is ready to talk.
  • Let people pick how they want to be contacted (call, WhatsApp, text or email).

Common questions

Can I have both a calculator and a contact form?

Yes. Many businesses keep a contact form for general questions and use a calculator for pricing enquiries.

What information does a calculator enquiry include?

The customer’s name, phone, email and notes, plus the job they picked, the size or option and the guide price they saw.
